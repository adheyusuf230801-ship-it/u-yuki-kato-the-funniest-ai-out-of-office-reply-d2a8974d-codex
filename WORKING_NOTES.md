## Selection

**Public title: Unpaid Training**

Winning line:

> I'm an AI on vacation. I told my backup to act human. It's suing us for unpaid training.

The first sentence establishes the required auto-reply immediately. The second gives a plausible agent instruction. The last makes successful human imitation unexpectedly expensive: the backup interprets model training as unpaid labor. That double meaning makes the machine's own operation carry the joke. “Us” also pulls the recipient into the consequence. The short ending needs no explanation inside the reply and avoids beach, inbox-zero, battery, and return-date cliches.

The scores favor this candidate because its punchline changes how the setup reads, rather than merely decorating an absence notice with technical vocabulary. Humor remains subjective; this is the strongest editorial choice, not a guaranteed result.

### Final decision

**Keep Stage A: Unpaid Training.** B1 and B6 offer fresh imagery, and B7 has a brisk payoff, but none beats the incumbent's immediate causal turn: ask a backup to behave like a human, receive a labor lawsuit. The double meaning of training is easy to recognize without technical background. Its human imitation causes the problem instead of merely supplying technical vocabulary for a joke.

Final line, unchanged from Stage A:

> I'm an AI on vacation. I told my backup to act human. It's suing us for unpaid training.

README.md still describes the selected line accurately, so it needs no change. Stage B rewrites main.py in full while retaining validation, the OUTPUT_DIR override with /output as default, and exact UTF-8 output without a trailing newline.
